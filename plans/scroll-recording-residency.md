# Bound the recording cache, not the declared list

**Proposal, not implemented.** An app should be able to declare all 20,000 Field Notes in the existing `column`. Keep their identities, layout, exact scroll range and keyed jumps. Make the expensive part proportional to what can be displayed or reused nearby. No list widget, row-height hint, pagination or scroll callback. This is a storage change on top of [visibility selection](scroll-paint-visibility.md) and [retained composition](retained-scroll-composition.md), not another layout engine.

## Where the memory goes

The actual 20,000-note page is **4,521,235 wire bytes**, 70,034 elements, 444,233 glyph slots, two ports, 5,000 flag badges and no images. The 7.13 GiB number is a *pre-allocation calculation*: 5.10 GiB for recordings/compiler plus 2.03 GiB for other workspace at default 80% headroom. Of the recording credits, 2.48 GiB is typed payload pages and 2.08 GiB is versions, ordinal trees and bindings. The five live reader slots protect old frames; this does not mean five copies were rendered. The upload got **no workspace grant**. Peak process RAM through the *failed* upload was about 605 MiB, including BEAM and staging. There is no rendered 20k RAM measurement.

**First change:** apply headroom once to raw demand; for keys use the allocator's actual capacity, including overlap of old and new identities. Separate live rebase scratch and detached before-images. Use small generation-checked key→slot handles for optional projected/recorded data instead of full inline values per key. Price the actual tables, including bucket rounding, simultaneous changes and growth; do not reduce the constants without changing the storage. Next, separate frozen side-specific headers from mutable builders. Batch immutable payloads in typed blocks rather than one heap allocation per tiny leaf where measurement justifies it; count block slack and whole blocks pinned by a reader. Remeasure the full declaration after each step. Don't start with EMRG compression, fewer readers or higher limits.

## Five viewports of reuse

Choose residency automatically at admission, by bytes rather than row count. If the whole order fits cheaply in the shared cache allowance, keep today's full reuse. Otherwise use the bounded policy below, with the **same** recording, replay and input code. No new public option.

- **Keep all layout, not all recordings.** Retain declarations, attachment generations, settled boxes and text/paragraph results, child order, scroll reach summaries and navigation for the whole tree. First layout and layout-affecting edits still pay for the declared content. Evict only reproducible drawing/hit programs, their cache references and associated derived state. Eviction is not unmount, blur, detach or an edit to the app.
- **Separate visible from worth keeping.** For a one-axis port of inner length `H` at offset `s`, the warm interval is at most `[s − 2H, s + 3H)`, clipped to content: **five lengths total**, not five extra on either side. Query the existing reach index in content coordinates. Use actual possible ink, hits, escaping descendants and shadow strips, not row starts or an average pitch. Only the ordinary visible selection is replayed and routable. The halo is permission to *keep*, not a command to draw, decode or prebuild five screens. On first demand record a missing side from settled inputs; retain an unchanged side for reuse. On return after an actual eviction, record it again. This last case deliberately relaxes the previous indefinite-return reuse guarantee.
- **Move the window by its differences.** On the port's existing Project job, reuse unchanged visible runs; retire just resident entries leaving the warm region. Prune empty ordinal branches and clear their mutable saved-program, standalone binding, material/fit-reader references as applicable. Seek directly to a jump destination; do not walk from the old offset or sweep all keys. Cache by attachment and independent draw/hit revisions, not by ordinal alone. An edited, reordered or remounted row must never revive a stale program. No second dirty queue or flattening on the render thread.
- **Share one byte-limited pool.** The nearest ordinary port owns the content. An outer port counts a nested shell once, not all its rows again. Release optional inner residency when the outer selection drops the branch. The port pin/chrome stays stationary; a flagged note moves with its own flag in the existing Nearby order. For biaxial or irregular content start with visible candidates and spend only spare bytes on nearby content; don't silently allocate a 5×5 rectangle per port. If pressure rises, give up the halo first. Never omit a visible contributor to satisfy a row count.

On the real **256-note** control at 584×506 and scale 1, the inner port is 176 px: its five lengths are **880 px**. A private query of the existing body/hit/shadow reach at **all 11,085 integer offsets**, with the outer page stationary, found at most **6 candidate note rows** for the normal port and **22 (78 elements including 6 flags)** for the expanded window. These are candidate sets, not render calls, a measured eviction implementation, or a general limit of 22. One row can itself contain thousands of glyphs.

## How to admit it safely

`5H` is a **distance**, not a byte bound. Before accepting an edit, reserve (1) compact whole-document/layout storage, (2) stationary output and the largest *mandatory visible* working set, (3) bounded optional warm storage, and (4) builder/replacement overlap and independently pinned visible versions. Size ordinary draw/hit buffers, scratch, local pages, ordinals and bindings from those distinct bounds on both [live](../native/emerge_skia/src/tree/wire/publisher.rs) and [detached](../native/emerge_skia/src/tree/wire/patch.rs) ingress. A cache miss must fit the already-admitted pool. A sealed frame pins the selected immutable subtrees plus its shell and inputs, **not a root that still owns the entire warm cache**. Five readers at five disjoint offsets must have room; never overwrite an installed frame to make room for the next.

Start with a provable case, not a `Field Notes` special case: a bounded-axis ordinary order with positive lower bounds on advance (not necessarily equal) and bounded protrusion. On the existing final-topology proof, count costs by subtree: commands, glyphs, hit/clip/pose data, material bytes, nested shells and export breaks. An axis-length upper bound plus protrusions and a lower bound on advance gives a conservative bound on intersected ordinals, including partly intersected endpoints. Price the **heaviest possible** such span, or use a safe per-row maximum times that bound capped by total demand; not the first screen's cost. Include the largest indivisible program, path-copy nodes and side-specific scratch. Maintain summaries on affected order/ancestry changes, not on scroll. In the notes fixture the 32/48 px rows and 4 px spacing supply advances, but the flags, shadows and text overhang still need pricing.

The proof must cover every reachable offset and supported resize/scale for its reservation. A bound relying on the current display must be reproved and funded *before* switching display state, not discovered to be too small halfway through a scroll. Where that cannot be guaranteed, charge those branches conservatively in full. Zero advance, unrestricted poses, unknown/nonfinite reach, fully overlapping rows or a huge row cannot be waved away by overscan. Use an exactly provisioned fallback if it fits; otherwise refuse the transaction before publication. Temporary pins can cause `:busy` (not accepted or queued); reader exhaustion on a previously accepted tree is seal backpressure, not an excuse to clip the output. No second layout solve in the publisher.

## Done when

1. The **unchanged 4,521,235-byte 20k EMRG** installs revision 1 and a correct first frame at **default 80% headroom and unchanged quotas**. Keep 256 as a positive control and zero headroom as a diagnostic. Separate wire bytes, credits, cold allocator requests, issued/reserved slots, first-frame time, and peak/steady whole-process RAM. No installed-RAM or FPS prediction from the credit formula.
2. Sweep end-to-end, jump directly 1 → 129 → 20,000 → 1, reverse just inside/outside the warm boundary, resize, and edit hidden content at equal bounds. Resident *program bytes* plateau rather than following all visited rows; cold/evicted rows rerecord, unchanged visible selections do not. Compare full frames, flags/pin/footer clicks and local coordinates. Keep layout, navigation/Tab reveal, capture/remount and raw inputs independent of cache residency.
3. Hold old scenes across disjoint jumps/edits; reproduce their pixels/hits and fitted-image versions after eviction. Exercise both streams independently, nested/horizontal ports, variable pitch, disjoint reach, overhanging/empty exporters, a single giant row and a genuinely over-limit visible set. Prove refusal/release/retry and no unreserved heap growth in accepted scroll work. Keep the existing full-reuse controls (including fitted glyph/image rows) and the tripled Cards warm move. Add a separately budgeted forced-eviction control; don't loosen an old assertion to simulate eviction. Measure first-use writes separately from replay. Run locked Cargo and Mix suites against their named baselines.
