# Scroll by composing retained local drawings

**Implemented.** Scrolling changes where content is displayed, not what the content draws. The tree retains local drawing and hit programs after Place, using the existing element and paragraph emitters. There is no text-list fast path or public opt-in.

## Before the change

In the native replay of Cards → View source at 584×506, moving from 30 to 31 px kept the same 20 paragraphs. With either one or three copies of the source, all five layout passes did zero jobs. Yet [`Layers::project`](../native/emerge_skia/src/tree/layers/projection.rs) ran 315 times: it pushed the scroll translation and inherited clip down through visible descendants. [`Output::collect`](../native/emerge_skia/src/tree/paint.rs) then examined 330 draw and 329 hit child entries; the sealed output contained 130 commands, 472 glyphs and 7 hits. The [inline collector](../native/emerge_skia/src/tree/paint/inline.rs) copied glyphs into the frame builder. Publishing the resulting `Arc<Drawing>` was cheap; constructing its contents again was the problem. These are work counts, not GPU timings. Local witness: `/workspace/.cards-scroll-why-vsIZyL/probe.log`.

## Ownership after Place

- **Layout** remains Context → Measure → Flow → Reflow → Place. Read settled placement to establish a child-to-parent offset when placement changes. Do not change layout geometry or schedule those passes for a scroll receipt.
- **Drawing** records an element's own ink around its local origin. A paragraph uses its settled fragments, preserving span owners, styles and local glyph positions. Do not bake in the screen origin, scroll offset, or today's viewport intersection: the first exposure may show only half a row. Record on first demand, keep the recording when it scrolls away, and reuse it on return. An edit to a drawing input (including inherited style or a replaced image) invalidates that version through the existing worksets. A hit-only edit does not. This cache is of commands and referenced resources, not a GPU texture per element.
- **Composition** owns ordered child references, relative transforms, clips and group opacity. A scrollport has a stationary clip and **one translated ordinary-content edge**. Scrollbar chrome and port-owned Nearby stay in port space; a row's Nearby moves with the row and can escape the port while the anchor is visible, unless explicitly clipped. Preserve visual band order, rounded clips, active-axis shadow guards and group blending; don't bake ancestor effects into child drawings. Keep the [visibility runs](scroll-paint-visibility.md) as slices of the retained order. A warm move checks the port window, updates the translation and thumb, and publishes only the changed composition path. Do not build a fresh array of handles for all visible children. Crossing a boundary changes the selection and materializes only missing or invalid recordings. Nested ports may need their windows checked if their exposure changes; uncertain reach is not proof of invisibility.
- **Sealing** pins an immutable composition root, its referenced drawing and hit versions, and frame metadata. A frame must work even if the previous frame was dropped. Nothing refers into mutable tree scratch; neither a scroll nor a later edit can mutate an installed reader. Share unchanged child orders and payload storage; no flat scene rebuild or deep equality scan on scroll. A changed visible offset is a changed picture even when all content handles match. Reserve storage for recordings and pinned versions before edit acceptance on both admission paths; recycle retired versions after their last reader. No second scheduler, NIF or wire change.
- **Rendering** replays the selected references directly, entering the port clip before applying the content translation. It can still draw every visible glyph on a fresh target; that does not require generating or copying those commands again. **Input** maps the point back through the *installed* transform chain and tests retained local shapes in hit order. Keep per-side visibility and blockers, attachment-qualified capture and route epochs; keep Tab order independent of pointer visibility. Do not generate a new screen-space hit list on scroll.

## Acceptance tests for the cutover

1. Start with the 30 → 31 px replay above. Require zero descendant projection jobs, zero content draw/hit recordings rebuilt, zero command/glyph/hit payload bytes copied into the frame, and no walk or copy of the visible token references to assemble it. Verify shared payload and child-order identity **and** changed pixels/local hits. Count port boundary probes, composition updates and renderer replay separately: moving a flattening pass to the draw thread is not a pass.
2. Scroll a partly visible row fully into view, expose a never-drawn row, leave, then return. Only the cold row may need its first recording. Edit that row's ink at unchanged bounds while hidden: its next exposure must show the new version. Keep far jumps sparse on the monotone list; retain the conservative fallback for unknown reach.
3. Compare pixels and hit winners with the exhaustive reference for nested/transformed ports, inline continuations, opacity and escaping Nearby. Hold or reject a frame: the old scroll position, local clicks and routes must stay installed until successful handoff. Keep reserved-heap, focus-reveal, binary headless and private Wayland controls. No change to the gallery or public diagnostics is needed to specify this contract.

## What changed

[Local programs](../native/emerge_skia/src/tree/paint/recording/local.rs) version an owner's ink and listeners separately from its placement and ordered children. A partly visible owner records its complete program at first use. Paragraphs reference their settled span owners and fragments; inherited brushes, fonts and image inputs outlive the compiler. A port's [frame bindings](../native/emerge_skia/src/tree/paint/recording/bindings.rs) select immutable ordinal ranges and carry the installed displacement. A hit-only edit can replace one side without changing the picture. A scroll can change the picture **without** changing the shell or either order root.

For a mixed order, ordinary spans remain calls into that order. A selected exporter or nested port breaks the span at its own ordinal; its admitted escaping roots enter the existing Nearby FIFO with their establishing plane, explicit guards and opacity ancestry. A directly borrowed bitmap mask, behind mount or inline contribution can share a recording with its host. When a topology edit moves that recording boundary, the geometry pass that compiles the new group releases the absorbed descendants' cache entries for that side. Otherwise moving a behind panel up a chain could keep a copy of the same glyph run at every former boundary. Sealed readers keep their own versions. A bitmap may borrow half a pixel from its nearest clipping host **only while the unrelaxed host intersects the exposure**. When the host ends exactly at the port edge, the image paints nothing. A rounded ancestor clips overlapping primitives *inside* a translucent group; it must not clip the composite twice. Selection uses the actual exposure. Nested ports may need a new window, and unknown reach may require inspecting all candidates. Only proven opaque interiors may suppress drawing, never hits. These are not universal constant-time claims.

[Fitted images](../native/emerge_skia/src/tree/paint/fits.rs) bind the chosen bitmap and crop on the tree owner, before sealing. With `decode_at_size: true`, revealing a larger use of the same JPEG can change a stationary thumbnail, too. On an insufficient target, the compiler reopens that source's previously sampled draw readers on the ordinary Compose/Project worksets before issuing the coalesced decode. It does not lay them out, retry layout until stable, or read the latest bitmap in replay. On a current failure only the contributing occurrences fail; a stale completion cannot overwrite a newer target. Held frames keep their own pixels. A sample stores either its direct source and crop or a fitted binding, not both: recording an image this way does not enlarge every command to carry both representations. The [growth regression](../native/emerge_skia/src/tree/tests/scrolling/visibility/content.rs) checks 48×32 → 160×105, success with zero LRU entries, a failed growth, a hidden thumbnail and return.

## Storage and verification

- Both publication and detached edits [reserve](../native/emerge_skia/src/tree/paint/recording/admission.rs) typed pages, ordinal nodes, source readers, fit slots, markers and overlapping frame versions before acceptance. Leaves are sized by aggregate payload and the number of nonempty recordings; a disjoint selection stores endpoints, not visible child handles. Held frames can refuse another seal. Releasing one permits retry without changing the other readers. [Allocator tests](../native/emerge_skia/src/tree/tests/allocations.rs) measured **0 alloc / 0 realloc / 0 free** after admission on the 1,024-row cold/warm/far/return trace, pinned-reader refusal/retry (one detached or five live), and shared-fit preparation. This excludes worker decode and renderer replay. It is **not** a promise that releasing an old bitmap never frees memory. In a mixed 68-row trace (33 disjoint runs), replacing 48×32 pixels with 160×105 in the single-reader bank made four frees during seal. Pinning **only** the old bitmap moved all four to its explicit drop; with a pinned live frame the seal made none. The post-decode refresh made none in either case. Both reader banks matched exhaustive pixels and fractional hit grids through growth, failed growth, cancellation and return.
- The coalescing and [source-reader directories](../native/emerge_skia/src/tree/paint/fits/readers.rs) reserve room for hash-table turnover as well as live entries. Replacing 112 fitted sources and remounting their readers for 100 generations used no additional heap calls in the private native probe. In 21 separate **recording-bank provisioning** samples the requested allocator bytes fit the admission credits; repeating an unchanged reserve needed no heap call. Sixteen cold **whole live workspace** measurements (eight demand vectors at 0% and 80% headroom) also fit their grants. Moving 23 behind panels from one sibling onto successive ancestors of a 1,260-glyph run needed no heap calls in the accepted refresh/seal intervals. These checks are not a measurement of bytes initialized, total process memory or GPU allocation.
- [Composition regressions](../native/emerge_skia/src/tree/tests/scrolling/recordings.rs) compare full rasters and local hits for nested ports, disjoint runs, transformed and rounded clips, overlapping opacity and multi-level exports. Remounting the same numeric ID does not revive old capture. Independently rebuilt **task-start** and final NIFs matched **73** full 120×65 RGBA buffers, including the 119 → 140 export boundary, and **10** full 80×40 bitmap/host-edge buffers. In a separate 240×50 binary headless viewport they matched **five** installed JPEG frames through wheel-driven growth and return, with clicks in both the port and the stationary thumbnail.
- Linux x86-64 locked release native tests: **364** default, **364** on Rust 1.91, **351** CPU-only, **388** Wayland/Vulkan-only, **364** stage-probes and **388** dual GL/Vulkan. Strict all-target Clippy passed on the five feature profiles. In the private BEAM builds: **37** focused input/scroll/Nearby tests each on ordinary, Vulkan-only and probe NIFs; **22** headless stage tests on probes; **4** diagnostics tests on ordinary. Rust/Mix format, warnings-as-errors compilation, Credo, Dialyzer, ExDoc and its local link check passed.
- **The ordinary Mix suite is not green.** The final seed-17 runs at normal concurrency and at `--max-cases 1` each had **930/969 passed, 39 failed, 21 excluded**, with the same failed and excluded names as their rebuilt task-start controls. Two earlier full runs of an intermediate candidate had **40** failures each: one additional 100 ms raw pointer-move timeout in `RetainedTreeSliceTest`, the other a raw button-press timeout in `BoundedFillTest`. Paired ordinary and serial runs of that source did not reproduce either addition; both affected files also passed together on the final NIF at seeds 17, 41 and 137. No assertion, exclusion or timeout was changed.
- The unmodified `../wayland_cards` passed **12 tests at each of two seeds** using its supplied offline dependencies. Private Sway seats, default Vulkan and explicit OpenGL, routed Cards → move a card → View source → scroll the tripled listing → Show cards. Full **584×506 installed/compositor RGB** matched and the moved card was restored. This does not demonstrate physical input, scanout, another platform or a fresh Hex resolution.
